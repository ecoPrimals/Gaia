Selected work — ecoPrimals, founder and lead architect, 2023 – present

A self-hosted scientific computing system in Rust, built by one person working with AI coding agents: 175+ published papers reproduced with stated numerical tolerances, 93,700+ automated tests, 43 public repositories, running on eleven machines I own. Open source, AGPL-3.0.

  • tideGlass, drug discovery. Rebuilt the GPS drug-screening platform (Bin Chen Lab, MSU; Cell 2026) from its published methods and primary data, on a task set by Andrea Gonzales (MSU Pharmacology & Toxicology). Seven science modules in one program, 220 tests; now being packaged and extended to neurofibromatosis.
  • Anderson-QS, biology and physics. Used the physics of waves in disordered material to model when a chemical signal spreads through bacteria or skin. Applied it to the published pharmacology of itch, using values from three of Andrea Gonzales's papers (359 consistency checks pass), and checked the mathematics against eleven papers. The model runs in a browser: primals.eco/static/anderson.html.
  • hotSpring, plasma physics and lattice QCD. Reproduced plasma simulations from Michael Murillo's group at MSU (nine of nine reference cases; five silent bugs found in the original code), then ran lattice QCD on consumer graphics cards without CUDA. NVIDIA and AMD cards agree to 6 parts per million; 40 to 53 times faster than the processor.
  • The platform underneath. Wrote the key management, encrypted networking between machines, content-addressed storage, cross-vendor graphics-card mathematics and provenance records that the science runs on, and self-host the web, mail and build systems.
