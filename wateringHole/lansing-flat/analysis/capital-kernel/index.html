The Capital Kernel — Dykema Gossett as Infrastructure

October 9, 2026

The Capital Kernel

There is a word for a system that persists across administrations, runs on both parties’ hardware, and provides the formation-compliance layer that all political operations depend on.

That word is operating system.

Dykema Gossett PLLC is Michigan’s political operating system. It runs from 201 Townsend Street, one block from the Capitol. It has been running since at least 1993.

Five Levels, One Firm

No other entity in Michigan occupies this complete vertical:

Level 1: Legislative

Dykema drafted Public Act 362 — the charter school law — in 1993. The law created the authorization framework that allows charter schools to operate outside traditional school board oversight.

Level 2: Judicial

In 1997, when PA 362 was challenged, Dykema defended it at the Michigan Supreme Court. The firm that wrote the law also protected it from constitutional challenge.

Level 3: Authorization

Dykema represents charter authorizers — the entities that grant and renew charter school operating agreements. The firm that wrote the law, defended the law, now represents the entities empowered by the law.

Level 4: Entity Formation

W. Alan Wilk, Dykema’s Team Leader of Political Compliance, has incorporated 7+ dark money vehicles from the firm’s Lansing office. Each one is a 501(c)(4) or LLC registered at or through 201 Townsend Street.

Save Detroit Jobs. Our Neighborhoods First. Detroit Jobs First. RFFW LLC. Put Progress First. Michigan First Principles. Peninsula Fund. All Wilk. All Dykema.

SDJ’s phone number — 517-374-9100 — is Dykema’s switchboard. SDJ’s compliance email is compliance@dykema.com. The IRS filing lists Dykema’s office phone.

Level 5: PAC Compliance

Renae Moore, Dykema’s Senior Compliance Specialist, manages the financial compliance for these vehicles plus the Federal and State PACs. On February 26, 2025, under oath in the Chatfield preliminary examination, she testified to managing 80+ accounts.

80+ accounts. One person. One desk at 201 Townsend.

The Bipartisan Proof

The Federal PAC (FEC C00342113) gave $131,000 to 47 candidates — both parties. The State PAC gave $725,000+ since 2017 — including $13,500 to Duggan and $250 to Yancey.

This is not partisan infrastructure. This is bipartisan infrastructure — and that is what makes it an operating system. It doesn’t care which party is in power. It provides the formation-compliance layer that both parties run on.

The Formation-Compliance Kernel

The kernel has two processes:

Formation (Wilk): Creates legal entities — incorporates 501(c)(4)s, files with LARA, registers agents, establishes bank accounts.

Compliance (Moore): Manages money flows — files FEC reports, files state campaign finance reports, handles IRS 990 filings, testifies when subpoenaed.

Together they form a factory: Wilk produces vehicles, Moore schedules their compliance. The factory runs continuously. The vehicles come and go — SDJ for one election cycle, ONF for another, Peninsula Fund for Chatfield. The factory itself is permanent.
